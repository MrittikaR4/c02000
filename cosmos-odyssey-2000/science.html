01 / OUR COSMIC NEIGHBOURHOOD

One star.
Many worlds.

Our Solar System formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago.

The Sun dominates its mass and provides the energy that powers much of the system's activity. Its gravity governs the motion of planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids and comets.

Rocky worlds occupy the inner Solar System. The outer regions contain giant planets, icy moons, rings and ancient small bodies. Each destination offers a different opportunity for scientific investigation.

SECTOR 01 / TERRESTRIAL WORLDS

Mercury

Why does Mercury experience extreme temperature changes?+

Mercury has almost no substantial atmosphere to retain and redistribute heat. Its sunlit surface becomes extremely hot, while the long night allows surface temperatures to fall dramatically.

Mission connection: NASA's MESSENGER orbited Mercury and investigated its surface, composition and magnetic field. ESA and JAXA's BepiColombo mission is designed to investigate the planet in greater detail.

NASA MERCURY ARCHIVE ↗ ESA BEPICOLOMBO ↗
Could water ice exist on the closest planet to the Sun?+

Yes. Water ice can persist in permanently shadowed polar craters that receive little or no direct sunlight. Mercury's proximity to the Sun does not mean every part of its surface is hot.

Design challenge: Plan observations of a permanently shadowed region while managing lighting, temperature and communication constraints.

SECTOR 02 / ATMOSPHERIC WORLDS

Venus

Why is Venus hotter than Mercury?+

Venus has a very dense carbon dioxide atmosphere that traps heat through an intense greenhouse effect. This makes Venus's surface hotter than Mercury's, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

Mission connection: NASA's Magellan used radar to map much of Venus's surface through its cloud cover. Venus Express and Akatsuki also investigated the planet's atmosphere.

NASA VENUS ARCHIVE ↗ ESA VENUS EXPRESS ↗
How can a spacecraft survive Venus's surface conditions?+

Venus has crushing atmospheric pressure and surface temperatures of roughly 465°C. A lander needs specially engineered electronics, thermal protection and pressure-resistant structures to operate there.

Design challenge: Choose the balance between protection, scientific payload, mass and operating lifetime.

SECTOR 03 / HABITABLE WORLDS

Earth

What makes Earth capable of supporting life as we know it?+

Earth has persistent surface liquid water, a protective atmosphere, suitable conditions in many regions and the chemical ingredients needed by known life. Scientists continue to investigate how these systems interact.

Mission connection: Earth-observing satellites measure oceans, ice, clouds, vegetation and atmospheric changes to help us understand our planet.

NASA EARTH SCIENCE ↗
How do satellites help us understand climate change?+

Satellites repeatedly measure variables such as sea level, ice coverage, surface temperature, atmospheric composition and changes in vegetation. These observations help scientists detect long-term patterns across the planet.

Design challenge: Select instruments and an observation schedule for a hypothetical Earth-monitoring mission.

SECTOR 04 / ANCIENT ENVIRONMENTS

Mars

What evidence suggests Mars once had liquid water?+

Ancient river channels, lake deposits, sedimentary rocks and water-altered minerals provide evidence that liquid water once flowed or collected on parts of Mars. This does not by itself prove that life existed there.

Mission connection: Perseverance explores Jezero Crater, investigates its geology and collects rock and regolith samples for possible future return to Earth.

NASA MARS ARCHIVE ↗ PERSEVERANCE ↗
Why is landing a spacecraft on Mars so challenging?+

Mars has an atmosphere, but it is thin. A spacecraft must survive atmospheric entry, slow down enough and deploy an appropriate landing system before reaching the surface safely.

Design challenge: Select a landing system based on payload mass, target terrain, risk and available resources.

SECTOR 05 / GAS GIANTS

Jupiter

What drives Jupiter's enormous storms?+

Jupiter's atmosphere contains powerful jet streams, turbulent cloud systems and enormous storms. Heat escaping from its interior contributes to atmospheric circulation. The Great Red Spot is a giant storm observed for centuries.

Mission connection: NASA's Juno spacecraft investigated Jupiter's atmosphere, magnetic field, gravity and interior.

JUNO MISSION ↗
Why is Europa a major target in the search for habitable environments?+

Evidence indicates that Europa has a global ocean beneath its icy crust. Liquid water, chemistry and energy are important ingredients when assessing habitability, but scientists do not know whether Europa hosts life.

Mission connection: Europa Clipper investigates Europa's ice shell, composition and potential habitability. It is not designed to detect life directly.

EUROPA CLIPPER ↗
What makes Ganymede different from other moons?+

Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System and has its own intrinsic magnetic field. Evidence also suggests that a large ocean exists beneath its icy surface.

Mission connection: ESA's JUICE mission is investigating Jupiter's icy moons, including Ganymede, Europa and Callisto.

SECTOR 06 / RING SYSTEMS

Saturn

How did Saturn's rings form?+

Saturn's rings contain countless particles made mainly of water ice. Their exact origin and age remain active areas of scientific research. Ring particles interact with Saturn's gravity and with nearby moons.

Mission connection: Cassini studied Saturn, its rings and its moons during an extended orbital mission.

NASA SATURN ARCHIVE ↗
Why is Titan scientifically fascinating?+

Titan has a thick nitrogen-rich atmosphere, lakes and seas of liquid hydrocarbons, and complex organic chemistry. Its surface chemistry provides a natural laboratory for investigating processes very different from Earth's.

Mission connection: NASA's Dragonfly rotorcraft mission is planned to explore Titan and investigate its chemistry and habitability potential.

TITAN SCIENCE ↗ DRAGONFLY MISSION ↗
Could Enceladus have conditions suitable for life?+

Enceladus ejects plumes containing water vapour and other material from beneath its icy surface. Cassini measurements provided evidence for a subsurface ocean and chemical ingredients of interest to habitability research.

Research question: What instruments would best analyse plume particles during a future mission?

SECTOR 07 / ICE GIANTS

Uranus

Why is Uranus tilted so dramatically?+

Uranus rotates with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees. A major collision early in its history is one proposed explanation, but scientists continue to investigate how this unusual orientation developed.

Mission connection: Voyager 2 flew past Uranus in 1986. A dedicated orbiter has been proposed for future exploration.

NASA URANUS ARCHIVE ↗
Why do scientists want a dedicated Uranus mission?+

A dedicated mission could study Uranus's atmosphere, interior, magnetic field, rings and moons over an extended period instead of during a brief flyby.

Design challenge: Plan a long-duration mission that balances power, communication distance, instruments and spacecraft reliability.

SECTOR 08 / DISTANT WORLDS

Neptune

What drives Neptune's powerful winds?+

Neptune has exceptionally fast atmospheric winds. Internal heat, atmospheric circulation and the transport of energy are important factors, although details of its weather remain under investigation.

Mission connection: Voyager 2 performed the only close flyby of Neptune so far, in 1989.

NASA NEPTUNE ARCHIVE ↗
What makes Triton an unusual moon?+

Triton travels around Neptune in a retrograde orbit, opposite Neptune's rotation. This suggests that Triton may have been captured rather than forming alongside Neptune. Voyager 2 also observed active surface features.

Research challenge: Choose instruments to investigate Triton's surface, thin atmosphere and possible internal activity.

SECTOR 09 / LUNAR EXPLORATION

The Moon

Why is water ice at the lunar poles important?+

Permanently shadowed regions may preserve water ice and other volatile substances. Studying them could reveal information about the Moon's history and help assess resources for future exploration.

Mission connection: Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter studies the lunar surface and environments relevant to exploration.

NASA MOON ARCHIVE ↗
How have Apollo, Artemis and Chandrayaan advanced lunar exploration?+

Apollo missions brought astronauts to the Moon and returned samples. Artemis is NASA's programme for renewed human exploration. India's Chandrayaan programme has used orbiters, impactors and landers to investigate lunar geology and polar regions.

Research challenge: Design a lunar science mission with a landing site, instruments and communications plan.

SECTOR 10 / SOLAR PHYSICS

The Sun

What causes solar storms, and why do they matter?+

The Sun's changing magnetic field can produce solar flares and coronal mass ejections. These events can affect satellites, radio communications, navigation systems and electrical infrastructure.

Mission connection: Parker Solar Probe studies the solar corona and solar wind. Solar Orbiter investigates the Sun and heliosphere.

NASA SUN ARCHIVE ↗ PARKER SOLAR PROBE ↗
Why is the Sun's corona much hotter than its visible surface?+

The solar corona reaches temperatures far above those of the visible surface. Scientists investigate how magnetic fields and plasma processes transfer energy into the corona; the details are an important open research problem.

Research challenge: Choose observations that could help distinguish between competing explanations of coronal heating.

SECTOR 11 / SMALL BODIES

Asteroids

What can asteroid samples tell us about the early Solar System?+

Many asteroids preserve ancient material from the Solar System's formation. Studying their composition can help scientists investigate the building blocks of planets and the history of water-bearing minerals and organic compounds.

Mission connection: NASA's OSIRIS-REx returned a sample from asteroid Bennu to Earth in 2023.

OSIRIS-REx ↗
Can a spacecraft change an asteroid's path?+

NASA's DART spacecraft struck Dimorphos, a small moonlet orbiting asteroid Didymos, in 2022. The impact changed Dimorphos's orbital period and demonstrated kinetic impact as a possible asteroid-deflection technique.

Design challenge: Investigate how impact speed, spacecraft mass, targeting accuracy and measurement uncertainty affect a deflection mission.

SECTOR 12 / ICY VISITORS

Comets

Why do comets develop comas and tails?+

As a comet approaches the Sun, its ices release gas and dust. This material forms a coma around the nucleus. Dust and ion tails are shaped by sunlight and the solar wind and generally extend away from the Sun.

Mission connection: ESA's Rosetta mission studied comet 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko and deployed the Philae lander.

NASA COMET SCIENCE ↗ ESA ROSETTA ↗

SECTOR 13 / DWARF PLANETS

Pluto & the Kuiper Belt

What did New Horizons discover about Pluto?+

NASA's New Horizons flyby in 2015 revealed mountains of water ice, nitrogen-ice plains, a complex atmosphere and a geologically varied surface. Pluto is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt.

Mission connection: New Horizons later flew past the Kuiper Belt object Arrokoth in 2019.

PLUTO ARCHIVE ↗
What makes Ceres different from other dwarf planets?+

Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and is classified as a dwarf planet. NASA's Dawn mission investigated its surface, bright deposits and evidence of water-related geological processes.

Research challenge: Compare the instruments needed to study surface minerals and possible subsurface water.

SECTOR 14 / MISSION ENGINEERING

Designing a Space Mission

How do engineers balance spacecraft mass, power and budget?+

Instruments and subsystems have mass, power, data and reliability requirements. Adding equipment can improve scientific capability but may increase launch costs, power needs and operational complexity.

Game challenge: Select instruments, allocate power, remain within a launch mass limit and reserve resources for communications and operations.

NASA LEARNING RESOURCES ↗
Why do distant spacecraft need communication planning?+

Signals take time to travel across space, and data rates depend on distance, antenna systems, transmitter power and ground-station availability. Spacecraft also need to manage data storage when they cannot communicate continuously.

Game challenge: Plan communication windows and choose which scientific data to prioritise within limited bandwidth.
